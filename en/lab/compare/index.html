Four strategies, the same rules of the game

Buy and hold, the trend filter, a grid bot and a DCA bot on the same BTC, over the same almost nine years and with the same fee. Every number uses the same formula and can be reproduced with the open-source code.

By Avelino González, WIQON Lab · Published November 13, 2026

Short answer

Over the full period the trend filter returned 42.7% a year with a −38.5% max drawdown; holding BTC, 23.3% with −81.2%. Consistency matters most: the filter returned 44.9% in 2018-2022 and 39.7% in 2023-2026, while holding went from 4.3% to 53.9%. The typical grid never got back to its starting capital.

How it was compared

  1. Same asset: Binance BTC/USDT spot, with 5-minute candles from the public archive, January 2018 to September 2026.
  2. Same conditions for all four: US$10,000 to start, a 0.1% fee per order and USDT earning nothing.
  3. Each strategy uses its experiment's simulator; the grid and the DCA bot in their typical settings.
  4. All are measured with the same definition of every metric, on the account value at each day's close.
  5. Control: before writing results, the comparison recomputes the published numbers of experiments #6 and #7 and stops if they don't match.

How each metric is calculated
Annual
Compound growth per year: (final value ÷ initial value) to the power of (365 ÷ days), minus 1.
Max drawdown
The worst fall in account value from a previous high.
Days in drawdown
The longest run of consecutive days below a previous high.
Volatility
Standard deviation of daily returns × √365.
Sharpe
Mean daily return ÷ its standard deviation × √365, with a zero risk-free rate.
Sortino
Like Sharpe, but the deviation only counts down days.
Calmar
Annual ÷ max drawdown: return per point of drawdown.
Positive years
Calendar years with a gain (2026 runs through September).

Drawdown from the high

Each point is the worst drawdown of its week, computed from the daily value.

What the comparison shows

  • The filter returned 44.9% in 2018-2022 and 39.7% in 2023-2026. Holding went from 4.3% to 53.9% and the DCA bot from 4.0% to 54.9%: their results depend on the market going up.
  • The DCA bot ended almost level with holding (23.4% versus 23.3% a year) with a similar drawdown (−69.4% versus −81.2%): without a stop loss it is buying BTC in more steps.
  • The typical grid ended at −13.8% a year after 8,832 closed round trips: each one gains a little, while the BTC bought on the way down loses a lot at once.
  • The filter lags holding in strong bull years (2023: +82.7% versus +155.6%); its edge is losing far less in bear years (2022: −20.8% versus −64.2%).
  • In this comparison USDT earns nothing. With USDT in Earn the filter returns more (experiment #3), but this way all four compete on equal terms.

Fact sheet · Experiment #3

Stage
Backtest: done · Demo: done (MetaTrader 5, Exness) · Real: in progress, not enough history to publish
Hypothesis
Holding BTC only while the daily close is above the 100-day average cuts the big drawdowns without giving up most of the upside.
Rules
  • Signal at the daily close: close > 100-day simple average?
  • Yes: 100% BTC. No: 100% USDT.
  • The order executes at the next day's open.
Asset and period
BTC/USDT · January 1, 2018 – September 30, 2026 · 1D
Data
5-minute BTC/USDT spot candles from Binance's public archive, no missing months; metrics are measured at each day's close (UTC).
Data fingerprint (SHA-256)

Costs and assumptions
0.1% fee per order. No separate slippage is added: the grid and the DCA bot use limit orders; the trend rule executes at the next day's open. USDT earns nothing (no Earn) in this comparison.
Benchmark
Buy and hold BTC, same window and fee (23.3% a year, −81.2% max drawdown)
Result measured in the comparison
42.7% a year · −38.5% max drawdown · Sharpe 1.05 · 111 orders (entries and exits)
Validation
  • 18 neighbouring parameter sets (50/100/150-day average, with and without volatility sizing): all between 39% and 57% a year.
  • Measured separately for 2018-2022 (two bear markets) and 2023-2026.
  • MetaTrader 5, CFD with real swaps (Exness demo, 2022-2026): x2.02 vs x1.96 for holding, 39% drawdown vs 67%. Swaps take about a third of the edge.
Caveats
  • It lags holding in strong bull years (2020, 2023 and 2024 in the comparison): its edge is protection in bear markets.
  • Few trades a year: the figures carry more uncertainty than their decimals suggest.
  • With USDT in Earn the result is higher (lab section 3); it is left out here so all strategies compete on equal terms.
  • One asset and one history: the future need not look alike.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the trend filter show 42.7% here and 56.6% in experiment #3?

Two reasons: there, USDT earns in Earn during the time out of BTC (about half), and the window starts on a different date, which is why holding differs too (37.3% there, 23.3% here). Here USDT earns nothing, so all four compete on equal terms. The conclusion doesn't change: it beats holding with less than half the drawdown.

Does it working in the past guarantee it will keep working?

No. It is one history of one asset, with few trades a year. What it does show is that it didn't depend on a lucky period: it returned about the same in 2018-2022 and 2023-2026.

Can I repeat the comparison?

Yes. The code, each experiment's fact sheet and the data fingerprint are on GitHub: python -m lab.comparador produces these tables and checks the published numbers.
